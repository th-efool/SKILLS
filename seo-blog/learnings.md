# SEO Blog Learnings

This file accumulates learnings over time. The `/seo-blog` skill reads this on every invocation.

## Seed Learnings

### From Google's AI Optimization Guide (2026)
- Google's #1 recommendation: unique POV with first-hand experience, not commodity content
- "Structured data isn't required for generative AI search" — FAQ schema helps rich snippets only, not AI overviews
- "You don't need machine-readable files (llms.txt) for AI search"
- "No requirement to break content into tiny pieces for AI" — self-contained sections are for readability, not AI parsing
- "AI systems can understand synonyms and general meanings" — don't write in a specific way just for AI
- Quality organization (clear paragraphs, sections, headings) is what matters
- No scaled content abuse — don't spam variations targeting query permutations
- RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) is how AI search works — it retrieves relevant pages from the index

### From AEO/GEO Panel
- Relevance > Domain Authority for smaller companies — better structure wins
- Content freshness increasingly important — update dates and stats
- Comparison pages with competitors drive GEO citations when users search those competitors
- Reddit/UGC: AI overviews pull heavily from Reddit — genuine participation matters
- YouTube: AI overviews increasingly cite video content
- Partner blogs and trade publications boost external citations
- Use AI to do semantic mapping for keywords — find what queries AI engines use

### From Content Structure Research
- TL;DR on top (bullets, <100 words) — many LLMs still don't parse prose TL;DRs well, bullets are safest
- Question-based H2 headings match search query format → rank better in AI overviews
- Self-contained sections: AI engines extract individual sections, not full articles
- Tables get pulled into AI overviews more often than prose
- Specific numbers ("47% improvement") build credibility with both humans and AI engines
- FAQ sections on host pages (pricing, features) → NOT standalone /faq page
- Each FAQ answer: 40-60 words, first sentence is the answer, then supporting detail

### From Humanization Research
- Burstiness (varied sentence length) is the #1 signal of human-written content
- Banned word list: delve, tapestry, pivotal, furthermore, moreover, etc. — strong AI detection signals
- Two-pass technique: generate for completeness first, then humanize in a second pass
- The H.U.M.A.N. framework: Hear aloud → Unique perspective → Mix it up → Anecdotes → Natural language

### From Reference Blogs
- reo.dev: blog as content ecosystem — guides, playbooks, case studies, academy all in one hub with category filters. Deep content (49-65 min reads) signals expertise.
- firecrawl.dev: clean minimal structure, launch weeks as marketing events, free tools for lead gen, integration pages at scale, explicit AI/agent user support
- Both use category-based filtering (not just chronological) for content discovery
- firecrawl's free tools (/tools/) are a smart lead gen + backlink strategy

### From Practical Experience
- Image generation prompts should be included in every outline — saves time at publish
- Interlinks should feel natural, not forced — link from relevant context, not "check out our blog"
- Glossary terms create a "topical moat" — 50+ terms drive massive aggregate traffic
- Comparison pages are highest-converting content type (~7.5% conversion rate)
- Reddit-sourced FAQs feel more authentic than assumed questions
